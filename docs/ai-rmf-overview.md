# NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF) — Overview

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0) was published by the U.S.
National Institute of Standards and Technology in January 2023. It is a
voluntary, non-sector-specific framework that helps organizations manage the
risks of designing, developing, deploying, and using AI systems. It is not a
checklist or a compliance regime — it is meant to be adapted to an
organization's specific context, risk tolerance, and AI use cases.

## Trustworthy AI Characteristics

The AI RMF frames "trustworthy AI" as AI that is:

- **Valid and Reliable** — performs as intended under the conditions of its
  deployment.
- **Safe** — does not endanger human life, health, property, or the
  environment.
- **Secure and Resilient** — withstands adverse events and can recover from
  incidents (including adversarial attacks such as data poisoning).
- **Accountable and Transparent** — the organization and its practices are
  visible to those interacting with the system.
- **Explainable and Interpretable** — the mechanisms of the system's
  operation can be described, and its outputs can be meaningfully
  interpreted by users.
- **Privacy-Enhanced** — safeguards autonomy, identity, and dignity through
  privacy-by-design practices.
- **Fair, with Harmful Bias Managed** — addresses harmful bias including
  systemic, computational/statistical, and human-cognitive bias.

No single characteristic guarantees trustworthiness on its own, and there are
often tensions between them (e.g. privacy vs. transparency) that require
context-specific tradeoffs.

## The Four Core Functions

The AI RMF Core organizes outcomes into four functions, described in detail
in `core-functions.md`:

1. **GOVERN** — cultivates a risk-management culture and cuts across all
   other functions.
2. **MAP** — establishes the context to frame risks related to an AI system.
3. **MEASURE** — employs quantitative, qualitative, or mixed-method tools to
   analyze and track identified risks.
4. **MANAGE** — allocates resources to address mapped and measured risks on
   a regular basis.

## Companion Resources

- **AI RMF Playbook** — a companion resource with suggested actions,
  references, and documentation guidance for each Core subcategory.
- **NIST AI 600-1 (Generative AI Profile)** — published July 2024, applies
  the AI RMF specifically to generative AI risks (e.g. confabulation,
  harmful content generation, data privacy, intellectual property).
- **NIST AI RMF Crosswalk** — maps AI RMF subcategories to other standards
  (e.g. ISO/IEC 42001, OECD AI Principles).
